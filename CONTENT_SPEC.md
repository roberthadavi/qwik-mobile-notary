## Legal rules that shape every page (Texas)
1. **Never use the words "notario", "notarios", "notaría" or "notario público" anywhere**, in either language, not even to explain the difference (Tex. Gov't Code §406.017). In Spanish write **"Notary Public"** (untranslated), "notarización", "notarizar", "servicios de notarización", "Notary Public móvil". The validator fails the file if the word appears. To explain the Mexico difference say: "Un Notary Public de Texas no es abogado ni equivale a la figura legal de México; no puede dar asesoría legal ni redactar documentos."
2. A notary is **not an attorney** and may not give legal advice, choose forms, or draft documents. Every page conveys: **you bring the document — we notarize it.** Point to free official forms where they exist. Never say "we prepare", "we draft", "document preparation", "we help you choose".
3. **Personal appearance**: the signer appears before the notary (in person, or by two-way audio/video for RON). Signer must be **willing and aware**. Never notarize a blank or incomplete document. Never notarize for a signer who is not present, and never backdate.
4. **Identification**: unexpired government-issued photo ID with signature (Texas driver license or ID, U.S. or foreign passport, military ID, Mexican consular ID/matrícula or Mexican passport are accepted photo IDs), or the notary's personal knowledge, or a **credible witness** who knows the signer and has no interest in the document.
5. **Acknowledgment vs. jurat**: for an acknowledgment the signer may have signed earlier and acknowledges the signature before the notary; for a jurat/oath the signer **signs in the notary's presence and swears** the contents are true. Most affidavits need a jurat. Leave affidavits unsigned until the notary arrives.
6. **Certified copies**: a Texas notary may certify copies only of documents that are **not recordable in public records and are not vital records** — never birth, death or marriage certificates, divorce decrees, deeds or court documents; those come certified from the issuing agency (El Paso County Clerk, Texas DSHS Vital Statistics, the court). A passport, diploma, driver license, letter or contract copy is fine.
7. **Home equity loans** (Tex. Const. art. XVI §50(a)(6)(N)): may be closed **only at the office of the lender, an attorney, or a title company** — never at the borrower's home. QWIK meets the borrower at that office. Texas home-equity loans also carry a 12-day notice and a 3-day right of rescission.
8. **Refinances** of a primary residence carry a 3-business-day right of rescission (Notice of Right to Cancel, TILA/Regulation Z).
9. **Weddings**: a notary commission does not authorize marriage ceremonies in Texas (Tex. Fam. Code §2.202). QWIK's officiant service exists because the officiant is **ordained/authorized** — describe it as an officiant service, never as a notary act, and never mention the notary seal on that page.
10. **I-9**: not a notarial act. USCIS lets an employer designate any **authorized representative** to complete Section 2 by physically examining original documents; the representative signs as a representative and **does not apply a notary seal**.
11. **Immigration**: do not present any service as "immigration documents" or imply immigration expertise. USCIS forms generally do not require notarization.
12. **Territory**: notarial acts only inside Texas. RON: the notary is in Texas; the signer may be anywhere.
13. **Wills** (Tex. Estates Code §251): a Texas will needs two credible witnesses age 14+; the notary's role is the **self-proving affidavit** (§251.104) or, for a holographic will, the testator's affidavit. **Advance directives** (Tex. Health & Safety Code ch. 166): Directive to Physicians and Medical Power of Attorney may be signed before **two qualifying witnesses or a notary**. **Statutory Durable Power of Attorney** (Tex. Estates Code ch. 752) must be **acknowledged before a notary**.
14. **Deeds** (Tex. Prop. Code §12.001) must be acknowledged before a notary to be recorded with the El Paso County Clerk. **Mechanic's lien affidavits** (Tex. Prop. Code ch. 53) are sworn (notarized) and filed with the county clerk by statutory deadlines.
15. **Signature by mark / physically unable to sign**: Texas allows a signer who cannot write to sign by mark, with the requirements of Tex. Gov't Code §406.0165 (witness). Notary refuses when the signer is not aware of what they are signing.
16. Honesty over hype: no invented statistics, no guarantees, no "guaranteed acceptance". Turnaround for government agencies is "typically" and realistic.
